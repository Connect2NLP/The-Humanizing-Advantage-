Why I Do What I Do, and Why I Love It So Much

The truth is firstly, it didn't begin in a training room or with a qualification. It began much earlier through everyday conversations, diverse life challenges and experiences, and an ever-growing curiosity about people.

In my early twenties, I found myself working two very different jobs. One was a regular work week, the other for fun and experience. During the day, I worked in sales and in the evenings, I worked in hotel restaurants. On the surface, I enjoyed both; however, they couldn't have been more different. One was driven by business, the other by hospitality. Yet they shared one thing in common.

People.

Every day introduced me to new personalities, different expectations, unique ways of thinking, and countless opportunities to communicate on different levels.

Whether someone was buying a product or enjoying a meal, success rarely depended on the product itself. It depended on how people felt. Did they feel heard? Understood? Valued? Respected? And, most importantly, was the outcome successful?

I did not realise this at the time, and looking back now, I know that those experiences quietly shaped the way I see the world today.

I've always loved learning, exploring new ideas and challenging myself in different environments. I'm energised by ambitious people, meaningful conversations and places and environments where growth is encouraged rather than expected.

But over time, my curiosity evolved. It became less about what people do, and much more about why and how they do it.

Why do two people experience exactly the same event and walk away with completely different conclusions?
Why do some people embrace change while others feel threatened by it?
Why do misunderstandings happen, even when everyone's intentions are genuinely good?

These questions became the beginning of a lifelong journey for me.

They led me to spend years studying human behaviour, communication, values, beliefs and the profound influence our thinking has on every decision we make.

I was eventually introduced to Neuro-Linguistic Programming NLP in 2011.

Admittedly, I did not quite enjoy some of it, only because my limitations about certain "things" were now starting to be challenged head-on. That said, I soon recognised this study's remarkable ability to transform the way I communicate, think and relate to myself and to others.

Within that same year, I qualified as an NLP Practitioner and soon after became a Master Practitioner, a path I never thought I'd take. I wanted more . . .

My passion for the field continued to grow, leading me to assist and facilitate transformational trainings throughout South Africa and the United States.

In 2017, and through the guidance, encouragement and support of Simon Gledhill, I earned my NLP Trainer certification in Las Vegas under the now-deceased Dr Tad James, a true legend, mentor and known Master in the field. This was a milestone that marked the beginning of a new chapter centred on teaching, coaching and creating meaningful impact.

One of the greatest insights I've gained over the years is this:

As humans, we don't experience the world as it is. We experience the world through the meaning we give it, some of which are,

Our values, and how they shape what matters to us.
Our beliefs, and how they shape what we believe is possible (or not).
Our experiences, and how they shape what we accept as true or false, and right or wrong.

What one person sees as a challenge, another sees as an opportunity.

What one person believes is right or wrong is often influenced by a lifetime of experiences, relationships, culture and perspective.

That doesn't necessarily make one person right and another wrong.
It simply reminds us that perspective matters.

The more I explored this idea, the more fascinated I became.
Not with changing people, but with helping people understand themselves more deeply.

Think about it like this: when people understand how they think, they begin to realise they have far more choices than they first imagined.

Although every story is unique, I've discovered something remarkably consistent. The challenges may look different on the surface... but underneath, they often share the same foundations. My work has never been about giving people a new personality or telling them who they should become. Instead, it's about creating the conditions where people can think more clearly, communicate more effectively and make decisions that align with who they truly are and the life they genuinely want to build.

One phrase I often return to is this:

People don't resist change. They resist being changed.

That belief has shaped everything I do. I don't believe transformation can be forced. I believe it can only be discovered.

When people feel psychologically safe...
when they're curious instead of defensive...
when they're invited rather than instructed...
lasting change becomes possible.

That philosophy became the foundation of The Humanizing Advantage Co. It is built on one simple belief: Every person already possesses strengths, potential and untapped capability.

Sometimes the greatest breakthrough doesn't come from learning something new. Sometimes it comes from seeing yourself differently.

If my work helps someone become a better leader...
build a stronger family...
create a healthier workplace...
inspire a young person...
strengthen a team...
or simply experience life with greater clarity, confidence and purpose...
then I believe we've achieved something meaningful.

Beyond the training room, I find equal joy in life's quieter and personal moments.

Classical music has been a lifelong passion, particularly the cello, which I actually played throughout my twenties. I no longer play, though listening to it remains a great passion.

I also have a deep appreciation for the outdoor, adventurous side of things, and for all types of plants and the natural world. They remind me that growth cannot be forced. It can only be nurtured with the right environment, patience and care.

Perhaps that's one of the greatest lessons human development has taught me.
People aren't so different.
Given the right conditions, we all have the capacity to grow.
And understanding people changes everything.

Julian Van Reenen

What I Believe

  • People are the strategy, not the process built around them.
  • The importance I give something determines other factors in life and it's important to be mindful of this.
  • Behaviour change is a skill, not a personality trait.
  • Communication is the foundation of every relationship, whether personal or professional.
  • Growth follows the same principles in business, families, education, sport, leadership and more.
  • Awareness creates choice, and choice creates change.
  • When we understand ourselves more deeply, we connect with others more meaningfully.

What if the greatest advantage you'll ever discover is not something you need to learn… but something you already possess?

People will rarely remember every word you say.
They will always remember how you made them think, how you made them feel, and how you helped them see themselves differently.
