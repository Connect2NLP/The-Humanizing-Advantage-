Why?

Julian's Journey

People often ask me where my interest in human behaviour began.

Why I Do What I Do

The truth is, it didn't begin in a classroom or with a qualification. It began much earlier, through everyday conversations, different life experiences and a growing curiosity about people.

In my early twenties, I worked two very different jobs. During the day I worked in sales, and in the evenings I worked in hotel restaurants. On the surface, they couldn't have been more different. One focused on business, the other on hospitality. Yet both had something fundamental in common.

People.

Every day brought new personalities, different expectations and unique ways of thinking. Every conversation required me to listen, adapt and communicate effectively. Regardless of the industry, success always came down to the same outcome, creating an experience where people felt understood, valued and satisfied.

Looking back, I didn't realise it then, but those experiences shaped the way I see the world today.

I've always enjoyed learning, exploring new ideas and challenging myself in different environments. I'm energised by ambitious people, meaningful conversations and environments where growth is encouraged rather than expected. But over time, my curiosity shifted beyond what people do and towards why they do it.

Why do two people experience the same event and walk away with completely different conclusions?
Why do some people thrive through change while others feel overwhelmed by it?
Why do misunderstandings happen, even when everyone's intentions are good?

Those questions led me to spend years studying human behaviour, communication, values, beliefs and the way our thinking influences every decision we make.

One of the greatest insights I've gained is that we don't experience the world as it is, we experience it through the meaning we give it.

Our values shape what matters to us.
Our beliefs shape what we believe is possible.
Our experiences shape what we accept as true.

What one person sees as a challenge, another sees as an opportunity.

What one person believes is right or wrong is often influenced by a lifetime of experiences, relationships and perspectives.

That doesn't necessarily make one person right and another wrong.
It reminds us that perspective matters.

The more I explored this idea, the more fascinated I became.
Not with changing people, but with helping them understand themselves more deeply.

Because when people understand how they think, they begin to recognise that they have more choices than they first imagined.

They communicate with greater intention.
They make better decisions.
They build stronger relationships.
They lead with greater confidence.
And they often discover possibilities they simply couldn't see before.

Over the years, I've had the opportunity to work with business owners, leaders, teams, schools, sports clubs, parents, families and individuals from many different backgrounds. Although every story is unique, I've found that the challenges people face often share the same foundations.

They come down to the way we think.
The meaning we assign to events.
The conversations we have with ourselves.
The conversations we have with others.

My work isn't about giving people a new personality or telling them who they should become.
It's about creating the space for people to think more clearly, communicate more effectively and make decisions that align with who they are and the life they want to build.

I often say that people don't resist change, they resist being changed.
That's why I don't believe in forcing transformation.
I believe in creating the conditions where people can discover it for themselves.

This philosophy has become the foundation of The Human Advantage.
It's built on a simple belief:
Every person already possesses strengths, potential and untapped capability.

Sometimes the greatest change doesn't come from learning something completely new.
Sometimes it comes from seeing yourself, the people around you and the opportunities ahead through a different lens.

If my work helps someone become a better leader, build a stronger family, create a healthier workplace, inspire a young person, strengthen a team or simply see life with greater clarity and purpose, then I believe we've achieved something meaningful.

Because at the heart of every business, every relationship, every school, every team and every community…
There are people.

And understanding people changes everything.

What I believe

  • People are the strategy, not the process around them.
  • Behaviour change is a skill, not a personality trait.
  • Communication is the foundation of every relationship, professional or personal.
  • Growth happens in the same places for a business, a family, and an athlete.

What if the greatest advantage you'll ever discover isn't something you need to learn… but something you already possess?
